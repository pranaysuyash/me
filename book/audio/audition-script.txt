No claim without evidence. Consider an airline ticket that clearly shows the passenger, flight number, origin, destination, and departure time, but does not show a terminal. A trustworthy system preserves that absence. It does not turn a blank field into Terminal Three merely because the answer sounds plausible. The model may propose a value, but the pipeline must ask a harder question: what evidence supports this claim, and what action is allowed when that evidence is missing? In this book, we will use evals, deterministic gates, human review, and release policy to build artificial intelligence systems that can explain what they know, what they inferred, and when they must stop.
