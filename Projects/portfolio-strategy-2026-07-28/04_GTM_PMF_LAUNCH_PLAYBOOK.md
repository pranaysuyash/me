# GTM, PMF and launch playbook

## PMF is a sequence of evidence, not a launch event

The correct sequence for this portfolio is:

1. **Problem evidence:** named operator, current workflow, frequency, cost, failure and workaround.
2. **Commitment evidence:** buyer supplies real data, introduces stakeholders or books a scoped working session.
3. **Payment evidence:** paid diagnostic or paid proof, even when manual behind the scenes.
4. **Outcome evidence:** time, error, turnaround, revenue, compliance or risk improves.
5. **Repeat evidence:** same customer repeats, expands or refers.
6. **Channel evidence:** the next customer can be acquired through a repeatable route.
7. **Product evidence:** repeated delivery can be standardized without destroying margin.

A public launch before stages 2–4 is a distribution experiment, not PMF.

## ICP selection rule

A good initial customer has:
- repeated work every week or month;
- sensitive or high-consequence documents/data;
- a named operator who feels the pain;
- visible current workaround;
- enough volume for an economic outcome;
- authority to pay for a bounded experiment;
- remote-accessible inputs.

Avoid buyers requiring field work, public-sector procurement before proof, or broad enterprise integration before a result.

## Founder-led outbound system

### Account research
Capture:
- workflow signal;
- role/person likely to own it;
- public evidence of volume or complexity;
- current tools;
- one reason the problem may be urgent now;
- one personalised proof asset.

### Message structure
- Observed workflow or risk.
- Specific bounded outcome.
- One sentence on why local/evidence-backed/human-reviewed matters.
- Offer to map one real packet or inquiry.
- Clear paid next step after fit is established.

### Discovery questions
- What arrives, from whom and in what formats?
- Who touches it and how many handoffs occur?
- Where do missing fields, wrong values or delays appear?
- How often does the workflow repeat?
- What happens when it is wrong or late?
- Which data cannot leave your environment?
- What would a successful 30-day proof change?
- Who pays and who must approve?

## Launch gates by project

### SignKit
Launch publicly when installer, payment, onboarding, recovery and support work; messaging avoids certified-signature claims; and at least one vertical demo exists.

### insurance_q
Launch only a safe bounded surface. Every material answer must cite source evidence or abstain. Advice-like screens must be removed/gated. Public D2C launch is secondary to institutional design partners.

### Waypoint
Do not launch the full OS as the promise. Sell the inquiry packet and follow-up workflow. Existing travel tools are integration targets, not enemies to replace.

### AdShot
Launch as a fixed output package after three products from one category meet fidelity and revision limits. Do not sell “unlimited AI creatives.”

### SentinelTwin
Launch a remote audit sample after integrator review and explicit assumptions/liability language.

## Metrics that matter

| Stage | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Outreach | qualified accounts, positive replies, booked discovery |
| Discovery | acknowledged pain, supplied real input, economic consequence, buyer authority |
| Offer | diagnostic proposals, close rate, days to close, objections |
| Delivery | turnaround, human minutes, correction rate, model/API cost, gross margin |
| Product | activation, repeated workflow, retention, expansion, support load |
| Channel | cost/time per qualified conversation, referral rate, repeatable source |

Do not use repository commits, features, screens, test count or documentation pages as commercial KPIs.

## Pricing method

Use a three-anchor test around the economic result. Do not price from token/API cost. Track:
- hours removed;
- errors prevented;
- turnaround reduced;
- revenue accelerated;
- risk/compliance evidence created;
- replacement cost of current tools and manual work.

Raise prices after proof, not after more features.
